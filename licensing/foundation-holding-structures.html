What Do Foundation & Holding Structures Involve?

BOOK A CONSULTATION ↗

Foundations and holding structures provide the architectural backbone for corporate group consolidation, international asset protection, risk ring-fencing, and multi-generational estate governance.

While holding companies act as corporate parent entities owning shares in operating subsidiaries, foundations offer a hybrid vehicle â€” combining the legal personality of a company (with no shareholders) with the wealth preservation benefits of a trust.

✦ THREE-TIER CORPORATE HIERARCHY

Interactive Master Holding & Foundation Pyramid

Select a tier in the corporate pyramid to analyze asset insulation and governance flow.

Tier 1: Foundation (DIFC / ADGM / RAK ICC)

The Foundation sits at the top of the group structure as an independent legal entity with full corporate capacity but no shares or equity owners. It holds the shares of the master holding company according to its Charter and By-Laws.

✦ Total protection against forced heirship laws and foreign inheritance claims
✦ Governed by a Foundation Council and supervised by a Guardian
✦ Perpetual structure capable of managing multi-generational legacy wealth
✦ STRUCTURAL COMPARISON

Foundations vs. Holding Companies

Key legal differences between an ownerless foundation structure and a traditional corporate holding company.

VEHICLE TYPE A

Foundation (DIFC / ADGM / RAK ICC)

Independent legal entity with separate personality but no shareholders. Created by a Founder and governed by a Foundation Council.

Ownership:Ownerless (No Shareholders)
Governance:Council & Guardian
Constitutional Doc:Charter & By-Laws
Succession Shield:100% Statutory Protection
VEHICLE TYPE B

Corporate Holding Company

Traditional corporate vehicle owning shares, equity, or real estate assets across operating subsidiaries.

Ownership:Shareholders (Equity Holders)
Governance:Board of Directors
Constitutional Doc:Articles of Association
Primary Role:Group Asset Consolidation
